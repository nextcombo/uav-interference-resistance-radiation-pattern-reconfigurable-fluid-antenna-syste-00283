# [UAV Interference Resistance] Radiation Pattern Reconfigurable Fluid Antenna System for Anti-Interference UAV Communication [with MATLAB Code]

1. Summary
The widespread use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) has spurred the development of advanced technologies for responding to unauthorized UAV flight. However, the resistance of legal UAVs against illegal interference has not been fully studied. This paper proposes an anti-interference UAV communication scheme based on a reconfigurable fluid antenna system (RPR-FAS), which integrates reconfigurable pixel antenna technology, allowing each antenna to have a tunable radiation pattern. Consequently, the RPR-FAS can enhance the angle resolution of UAV antennas when limited in number, thereby improving spectral efficiency (SE) and interference resistance.
2. Introduction
The widespread application of UAVs has raised concerns about unauthorized flights, leading to the rapid development of anti-UAV technologies. For example, passive perception methods have been proposed to acquire UAV model parameters, and methods using co-channel base station communication signals to interfere with unauthorized UAVs have also been developed. Various monitoring technologies have been used to enhance the effectiveness of anti-UAV interference measures.
However, as anti-UAV technology advances, new issues arise: legitimate UAVs may encounter misfire or malicious interference, necessitating improvements in their perception capabilities to detect and counteract potential interference. Unfortunately, civilian UAVs are constrained by factors such as size, endurance, cost, and practicality, which inherently limit their perception capabilities. Moreover, UAVs typically operate in industrial, scientific, and medical (ISM) bands, where wavelengths are long and the limited physical space of UAVs makes it difficult to install large antenna arrays for effective angular perception.
Recent studies have shown that fluid antenna systems (FAS) can enhance the perception capabilities of limited aperture arrays. A FAS is a flexible reconfigurable antenna system capable of changing the shape, position, and radio frequency characteristics of its radiation pattern, achieving spatial diversity and flexibility. By adjusting the radiation pattern of the FAS, even with a small number of antennas or even a single antenna, precise detection of spatial electromagnetic signals can be achieved. Therefore, deploying FASs on UAVs can significantly improve their perception capabilities, which is an important prerequisite for enhancing spectral efficiency.
Existing research on FAS perception methods still needs to be improved to enhance estimation accuracy and reduce overhead, and some FAS-related studies are not applicable to reconfigurable radiation pattern systems. Meanwhile, existing UAV interference mitigation research focuses mainly on spectrum perception and resource coordination, lacking in scenarios where UAVs encounter misdirection or malicious interference. Based on this, this paper conducts research on Integrated Perception and Communication (ISAC) based on RPR-FAS, aiming to endowed UAVs with detection and interference resistance capabilities.
III. Conclusion This paper addresses the challenge of illegal interference faced by legal UAVs in the low-altitude economy driven by widespread applications, proposing an anti-interference UAV communication scheme based on RPR-FAS. This scheme is based on reconfigurable pixel antenna technology, which can improve the UAV's angle perception capability and spectral efficiency even in a single antenna scenario, and has strong generality, requiring only the replacement of the radiation directional gain matrix with an actual one for application.
Specifically, this paper proposes LSO-OMP-MMV algorithm and maximum likelihood estimation method based on the law of large numbers, respectively used to estimate the arrival angles of UAV controllers and interference sources; through Fourier transform of the radiation directional gain matrix, the storage overhead of LSO-OMP-MMV algorithm is reduced by 1-2 orders of magnitude. After the perception phase, the optimal uplink antenna radiation pattern and combiner are obtained using alternating optimization method, combined with exhaustive search and waterfilling algorithm to obtain the optimal downlink antenna radiation pattern and beamformer.
Simulation results verify the effectiveness of the proposed scheme, showing that the RPR-FAS has advantages in terms of spectral efficiency compared to fixed directional antenna systems even in the absence of interference. Although this scheme is primarily targeted at remotely controlled UAVs subjected to illegal interference, it can be easily extended to other scenarios such as networked UAVs facing illegal interference or remotely controlled UAVs experiencing unintentional interference.
